# Market Knowledge Notes

## End-of-day prices
End-of-day stock prices are prices from the most recent completed trading session.
They are useful for general research, but they are not the same as live intraday
or real-time quotes.

## Open, high, low, and close
The open is the first traded price in a session. The high is the highest traded
price. The low is the lowest traded price. The close is the final traded price
or official closing price for the session.

## Volume
Volume is the number of shares traded during a session. Higher volume often means
there was more market activity in that ticker, but volume alone does not indicate
whether a stock is a good investment.

## Adjusted close
Adjusted close is a historical price adjusted for corporate actions such as stock
splits and dividends. It is commonly used for comparing historical performance.

## Ticker symbols
A ticker symbol is a short code used to identify a publicly traded security. For
example, AAPL refers to Apple Inc. and MSFT refers to Microsoft Corporation.

## Exchange codes
Stock data providers often include exchange identifiers. XNAS commonly refers to
Nasdaq, XNYS to the New York Stock Exchange, XTSE to the Toronto Stock Exchange,
and XLON to the London Stock Exchange.

## Global listings
The same company may trade on more than one exchange or may have related listings
in different countries. Prices may be quoted in different currencies depending on
where the security trades.

## Currency caution
Ticker comparisons should consider currency. A stock quoted in USD cannot be
directly compared with a stock quoted in CAD, GBP, EUR, or JPY without considering
exchange rates.
